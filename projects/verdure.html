Overview

My Role

Developed in collaboration with FlexiSpot, verdure is an integrated system that pictures household indoor planting in a flexible way through modularity. With verdure, we promote a greener, more sustainable lifestyle within the context of urban living households.

Our group was the second place of 30+ participants and got RMB 12,000 award.

I led the translation of research findings into tangible solutions.

​

UX/UI design — app flows and screen, and how to match research findings with required technology

Product design — ideation, sketching of the physical product

System design — how the shelf and the app talk to each other

Plants brings sense of belonging to home

Care is easy to forget

insights from research

Space and light limit where plants can go

People grow plants to decorate their homes, get closer to nature, and feel calmer. In compact homes, greenery can also visually divide living, dining and work areas.

Heavy soil pots and shelves mean bending and lifting, which is especially tiring for the elderly and corporate worker who sits long daily. Each plant has different needs, and people often forget to cater.

Light is essential, but window orientation and balcony size restrict placement especially in apartments. Shelves need to be sturdy and easy to move, and soil raises worries about mess and pests.

Storyboard

01

After her evening yoga session, Ning returns home, calm and energized. She slips off her sneakers, takes out her earbuds, and lets out a quiet sigh of contentment.

02

Her eyes are naturally drawn to the Verdure Planting unit standing gracefully between the living and dining areas — her daily green companion.

03

Heading into the kitchen, she starts planning her post-workout treat: a refreshing veggie smoothie and a protein-packed salad.

04

She opens the fridge, scanning for ingredients, but hesitates — What greens should I use today?

05

Just then, her phone buzzes. A notification from the Verdure app: “Your kale is ready to harvest!”

06

She grabs her favorite harvest bowl and walks over to the Verdure Planting unit.

07

She folds down the small workstation tray, placing her kitchen shears and bowl neatly on the surface.

08

With a tap in the app, she adjusts the height of the garden.

09

Soft mechanical sounds follow — the entire frame gently lowers to a comfortable working position.

10

Ning snips off a few lush, perfectly grown kale leaves.

11

Later, at the dining table beside her vertical garden, she digs into her salad and smoothie combo — fresh, colorful, and powered by her very own indoor farm.

WHAT I WOULD DO DIFFERENTLY

Building the 1:1 prototype early made ergonomic and interaction problems visible that drawings had hidden. Next time I would test the planting itself, watering, drainage and light using the shelf, not only the furniture interaction. I would also start from a smaller MVP and check whether people would pay for the core value before building the full integrated system. Lastly, it would be great if the UI interfaces could be tested too
